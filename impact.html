Our Impact

At MoneyMind Games, we make financial literacy accessible for kids, parents, and educators through fun classroom and library showcases. By sparking curiosity and building confidence, we help students and families develop financial skills and start meaningful conversations about money. Through our train-the-trainer model, we equip educators to expand financial literacy in their classrooms.

Classroom Showcases

Our classroom showcases bring InvestQuest directly to students, turning financial literacy into an interactive, hands-on experience. We guide kids through the game, then give them the space to play, collaborate, and problem-solve together. At the end of the presentation, we invite students to share what they’ve learned, sparking conversations about budgeting, investing, and decision-making that connect directly to their lives. These showcases don’t just teach financial concepts; they empower young minds to think critically, build confidence with money, and carry the lessons they’ve learned beyond the classroom.

Library Events

Our library events go beyond just introducing InvestQuest, they foster connections within the community. By working with local libraries, we create an accessible space where families, students, and educators can come together to explore financial literacy in a fun and engaging way. Each event sparks conversations about saving, investing, and planning for the future, while also encouraging collaboration between both parents and their kids and educators and their students. Families have the opportunity to learn side by side, students discover practical skills beyond the classroom, and libraries strengthen their role within the community as centers of learning and positive change.

Educator Workshops

Our educator professional development sessions build a collaborative network of educators across San Diego committed to advancing financial literacy. We provide hands-on training in how to play and implement InvestQuest in the classroom, and we equip educators with our self-developed 8-lesson curriculum. Teachers leave with practical tools they can apply immediately, so schools and expanded learning programs can deliver consistent financial education.
